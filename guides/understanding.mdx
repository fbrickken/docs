In Brickken, each offering results in the creation of a custom on-chain token that represents a specific RWA, governed by a smart contract and operated by the issuing company (the tokenizer)."

## Types of Tokenized Assets
Depending on the structure defined by the issuer, a token can represent:

- Equity Tokens: Represent shares in a company, entitling the holder to dividends and voting rights (when applicable).

- Debt Tokens: Represent a financial obligation (like a bond or loan), where the issuer agrees to repay the investor with interest.

- Revenue-share Tokens: Entitle the holder to a percentage of revenues generated by a project or business line.

- Utility Tokens: Grant access to a product, platform, or service but do not carry financial claims.

> ⚠️ Always read the documentation provided by the issuer to understand the rights and risks associated with each offering.

## How Are These Tokens Created?
Each token is deployed through Brickken’s audited smart contracts on the blockchain. When an offering is created:

- A custom ERC-20 compatible token is deployed

- The supply and conditions are fixed according to the issuer’s setup

- Investors receive these tokens directly in their wallets upon successful investment and verification

## Why Tokenized RWAs Matter
Tokenized assets combine the benefits of traditional finance with the efficiency of blockchain technology:
| Traditional Assets         | Tokenized RWAs in Brickken                   |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Paper-based or centralized | Fully digital and decentralized              |
| Manual investor tracking   | On-chain, real-time cap table                |
| Complex compliance         | Automated KYC & AML workflows                |
| Limited liquidity          | Designed for eventual peer-to-peer transfer  |
| Costly and slow            | Fast, transparent, and programmable          |
